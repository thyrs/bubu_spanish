
In spanish all words have an accent. It means that part of the word sound harder. 
Words are divided in syllable.. which are ussally the combination of 2-3 letters:
Ca-sa (house) has 2 syllables second: "ca" and the fisrt: "sa" (counts from right to left)
If the accent is not marked with a ' (tilde) the accent will be on the second syllable(most of the time)
Tilde (') is only added to the vowels: á, é, í, ó, ú

1. (Stress on the last syllable)
Accent Rule:
They carry a written accent (tilde) if they end in a vowel (a, e, i, o, u), n, or s.
No accent if they end in any other consonant (like r or d).

Examples:
Sofá (so-FA) – ends in a vowel, so it has an accent.
Canción (can-CION) – ends in n, so it has an accent.
Comer (co-MER) – ends in r, no accent.
Tip: Think of agudas as words that "peak" at the end.

2. (Stress on the second-to-last syllable)
Accent Rule:
They carry a written accent if they do not end in a vowel, n, or s.
No accent if they end in a vowel, n, or s.

Examples:
Árbol (AR-bol) – ends in l, so it has an accent.
Lápiz (LA-piz) – ends in z, so it has an accent.
Casa (CA-sa) – ends in a vowel, no accent.
Sol (SOL) – ends in l, has an accent.
Tip: These are the most common stress patterns in Spanish, so they’re like the "default" setting.

3. (Stress on the third-to-last syllable) (or earlier, in rare cases).
Accent Rule:
Always carry a written accent, no matter how they end.

Examples:
Música (MU-si-ca) – stress on the third-to-last syllable, always accented.
Teléfono (te-LE-fo-no) – stress on the third-to-last syllable, always accented.
Tip: These are less common but easy to spot because they always have a tilde.
